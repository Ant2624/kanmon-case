How to evaluate

How you should compare embedded capital options.

An evaluation frame for partnership and procurement reviews. Use the questions, not the logos, to pressure-test fit.

You usually compare lender-of-record clarity, in-product control, product shelf for your cash gap, and commercial upside.

Ask every vendor

  • Who is lender of record?
  • Does the offer stay in our product under our brand?
  • Which cash-gap products fit our workflow?
  • What do we earn, and what risk do we keep?

Named comps you hear in the room.

Fair category framing. Features move fast; use these as diligence prompts, not a final feature matrix.

Parafin

Market-leading embedded capital for large platforms and marketplaces. Pressure-test lender-of-record structure, invoice/AP depth, and white-label control for your vertical workflow.

Stripe Capital

Strong if your customers already live in Stripe payments. Ask whether offer control and non-payments cash gaps still fit if capital is payments-tied.

Pipe

Receivables / revenue-linked capital. Compare underwriting model and whether your trigger is an unpaid invoice object vs trailing revenue.

Capchase

Often associated with SaaS growth financing. Compare fit for SMB operating cash gaps vs ARR-style use cases.

Bank referral / do nothing

Lowest build cost. Weakest capture of the cash gap inside your product. Customers leave your software; you usually earn little or nothing from that financing.

When Kanmon is the stronger fit.

Strong fit

  • You want one lender accountable end to end
  • Your customers hit cash gaps in invoices, AP, or ops software
  • You need white-label under your brand
  • You do not want to become a lender or carry credit risk

Look harder elsewhere

  • Your stack is payments-native and already capital-enabled
  • You only need a thin referral, not an embedded program
  • Your buyers need a product Kanmon does not offer for your segment
