Products

Six products. Six moments.

Every product maps to a cash gap your customers already hit inside your platform: term loans, lines of credit, invoice financing, accounts payable financing, and two concept directions, factoring and pay later.

Invoice financing

The buyer pays in 60 days. Payroll is Friday.

Who it is for: platforms whose customers carry receivables. Invoicing tools, EDI and supply chain software, staffing, any product where customers track unpaid invoices.

How it appears: eligible invoices show a financing option directly in the invoice list, at the row that is causing the pain.

Fact: soft credit pull only. Applying does not affect your customers' credit scores.

Accounts payable financing

The supplier bill lands before the inventory sells.

Who it is for: supply chain, commerce, and ERP platforms whose customers buy inventory ahead of revenue.

How it appears: a pay-now option on the bill itself, repaid over time in fixed installments.

Fact: your platform does not need embedded payments. Payment data helps underwriting but is not required.

Term loan

One big purchase stands between them and the next stage.

Who it is for: practice management and business management platforms whose customers face equipment, buildout, or expansion costs.

How it appears: a fixed offer with an amount and a term, and a short application.

Fact: funds in as little as 24 hours, typically within two days.

Line of credit

Cash flow is lumpy. The buffer should not be.

Who it is for: any platform whose customers face recurring timing gaps between work done and cash received.

How it appears: an available-credit widget on the dashboard, drawn as needed and repaid in fixed installments.

Fact: credit limits that grow with the business.

FactoringConcept

Not one stuck invoice. All of them.

Who it is for: staffing, logistics, and wholesale platforms whose customers invoice constantly and want the whole ledger working, not one rescue at a time.

How it appears: customers enroll once. From then on, new invoices fund automatically as they are issued, and repayment settles as buyers pay.

Why it fits: the same partner data that powers invoice financing, applied continuously.

Pay laterConcept

The purchase is now. The revenue is later.

Who it is for: B2B marketplaces and commerce platforms whose customers buy inventory, parts, or supplies at checkout.

How it appears: a pay-over-time option beside the pay-now button, in fixed installments, decided in seconds.

Why it fits: the moment of need and the moment of purchase are the same click.

And for supply chain platforms.

Programs can extend further still, including trade financing and purchase order financing. As a business grows into a program, existing financing can be consolidated into it, so limits scale up instead of stacking loans side by side.

The two products marked Concept are directions this redesign proposes, not announced Kanmon products.
