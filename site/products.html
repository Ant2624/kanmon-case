Products

Four live products. Two coming soon.

Customers hit cash gaps in your workflow. They leave for a bank, a broker, or nowhere, or they stall work until cash shows up. Four live products put an offer in that workflow so they keep moving, stay with you, and become your revenue: term loans, lines of credit, invoice financing, and accounts payable financing. Factoring and pay later are coming soon.

Invoice financing

The buyer pays in 60 days. Payroll is Friday.

For you if: your customers carry receivables. Invoicing tools, EDI and supply chain software, staffing, any product where customers track unpaid invoices.

How it appears: eligible invoices show a financing option directly in the invoice list, at the row that is causing the pain.

Fact: soft credit pull only. Applying does not affect your customers' credit scores.

Tick anything relevant. Your partnerships manager starts on it while you browse.

Accounts payable financing

The supplier bill lands before the inventory sells.

For you if: your customers buy inventory ahead of revenue on supply chain, commerce, or ERP tools.

How it appears: a pay-now option on the bill itself, repaid over time in fixed installments.

Fact: your platform does not need embedded payments. Payment data helps underwriting but is not required.

Term loan

One big purchase stands between them and the next stage.

For you if: your customers face equipment, buildout, or expansion costs.

How it appears: a fixed offer with an amount and a term, and a short application.

Fact: funds in as little as 24 hours, typically within two days.

Line of credit

Cash flow is lumpy. The buffer should not be.

For you if: your customers face recurring timing gaps between work done and cash received.

How it appears: an available-credit widget on the dashboard, drawn as needed and repaid in fixed installments.

Fact: credit limits that grow with the business.

FactoringComing soon

Not one stuck invoice. All of them.

For you if: your customers are in staffing, logistics, or wholesale, invoice constantly, and want the whole ledger working, not one rescue at a time.

How it appears: customers enroll once. From then on, new invoices fund automatically as they are issued, and repayment settles as buyers pay.

Why it fits: the same data from your product that powers invoice financing, applied continuously.

Pay laterComing soon

The purchase is now. The revenue is later.

For you if: your customers buy inventory, parts, or supplies at B2B checkout.

How it appears: a pay-over-time option beside the pay-now button, in fixed installments, decided in seconds.

Why it fits: need and purchase land on the same click.

And for supply chain platforms.

As a business grows into a program, existing financing can be consolidated into it, so limits scale up instead of stacking loans side by side. Some supply-chain programs can also explore trade or purchase-order financing later; those are not generally available yet.
